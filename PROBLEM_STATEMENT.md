# Problem Statement: Making Cyber Investigation Accessible for Bharat

## The problem is national, but the response is not yet accessible

India's digital transformation has brought payments, banking, commerce, public services, education, and small businesses onto connected platforms at extraordinary scale. That progress is a defining strength of Bharat's economy—and a growing point of exposure. A citizen may receive a bank-impersonation message over SMS or WhatsApp, be sent to a counterfeit UPI or KYC page, and lose money or account access before anyone has connected the warning signs. A small exporter or retailer may face a fake invoice, compromised business email, or payment-diversion attempt without an in-house security analyst to investigate it. Government departments and financial institutions, meanwhile, must maintain trust in services used by people across cities, towns, and villages.

The proposed challenge framing highlights **622M+ internet users in India at risk**, **₹8,000+ crore in annual losses to cyber fraud**, **240,000+ cyber incidents reported annually to CERT-In**, **63M+ SMEs with no dedicated cybersecurity team**, and an estimate that **only 40% of criminal cases are resolved**. Together, these figures describe a country where digital exposure is broad, specialist response capacity is uneven, and the path from a suspicious signal to an evidence-backed investigation can be too slow. They are not all directly comparable measures: the reporting period and definitions of “internet user,” “loss,” “incident,” “SME,” and “resolved case” differ by source. They should be presented as challenge baseline figures and verified against the latest official or independently credible source before being used as audited national statistics.

## Why this matters specifically for India

**Digital India depends on public trust.** As more citizens rely on digital identity-linked services, online banking, direct benefit delivery, e-commerce, and digital payments, a cyber incident can undermine confidence well beyond the organization first attacked. Robust cyber defense is therefore part of making Digital India dependable and inclusive—not only a technical concern for large enterprises.

**The attack surface is shaped by how people and businesses transact here.** UPI and QR-code payment flows, bank and NBFC communications, mobile-first account access, Aadhaar- or KYC-themed lures, and high-volume messaging create familiar channels for impersonation and credential theft. Common patterns include fake “account blocked” notices, fraudulent collect requests, counterfeit customer-support numbers, malicious links shared through messaging apps, and cloned delivery or government-service pages. For businesses, invoice manipulation, supplier impersonation, stolen email credentials, and fraudulent changes to bank details can interrupt cash flow and operations. These examples are not unique to India, but their scale and fit with everyday Indian digital behavior make timely local investigation especially important.

**The stakes are amplified by transaction volume.** India's fintech and banking ecosystem handles **₹5.5T+ in transactions per year** in the challenge framing. That figure is intended to communicate the scale of activity exposed to fraud and service disruption; transaction value is not itself a measure of fraud loss or money that this product can claim to protect. Even a small fraction of users or businesses affected can mean substantial financial and social harm.

**Most organizations cannot buy a traditional SOC.** Banks and large technology firms can employ security operations teams, but a small business often cannot justify dedicated analysts, multiple security products, and round-the-clock monitoring. Indicative challenge estimates put even a modest initial setup at around **₹50,000**, with ongoing SOC or managed-security costs of **₹1–2 lakh per month**; actual prices vary considerably with coverage and provider, and a full SOC may cost more. For a small manufacturer, retailer, clinic, logistics operator, or local service business, those recurring costs compete directly with core operating needs. The result is a two-tier defense landscape: well-resourced institutions can investigate, while many smaller organizations must rely on general IT staff, delayed external help, or no formal response process.

Cybercrime directly affects millions of Indian citizens—not just corporate networks. A compromised account can expose personal information, interrupt access to savings, or make a victim vulnerable to repeat targeting. Fraud can also affect families who depend on digital payments, small traders with limited working capital, and customers whose trust is essential to online commerce. Detection is only one part of the response, but slow, fragmented evidence review can delay account protection, escalation, and recovery.

## Who needs a more accessible investigation capability?

- **Indian banks and NBFCs** handling large volumes of customer communications and suspicious links, and needing consistent, reviewable first-line investigation.
- **Fintech startups**, including a potential target cohort of **500+** firms, that need to grow security operations without building a large SOC for every team.
- **Government IT departments** responsible for protecting public-facing digital services and routing credible incidents to authorized responders.
- **SMEs and mid-market companies** that need practical security triage without the price and staffing requirements of a traditional SOC.
- **Law enforcement and cybercrime investigators**, where applicable, who may benefit from structured, traceable evidence summaries to support investigation handoffs. The agent is not a replacement for police investigation, forensic standards, or judicial processes.

## The investigation bottleneck

When a phishing or suspicious-activity alert arrives, relevant evidence may be scattered across an email's headers and attachments, embedded URLs, threat-reputation sources, browser history, and endpoint activity. An analyst must collect and interpret these signals, decide whether they relate to the same event, document the reasoning, and recommend next steps. Manual triage can consume **25–45 minutes per incident** in the project's current problem framing. At scale, repetitive review creates queues, inconsistent decisions, and less time for complex cases. For organizations without specialists, the barrier is more basic: they may not know what evidence to preserve or where to send it.

Forensic Engine is designed to address this specific first-response gap. It can analyze supported evidence such as suspicious URLs, `.eml` email, text alerts, and browser-history databases; follow leads such as links found in an email; correlate evidence; and produce an auditable investigation report with actionable recommendations. It can help a human reviewer prioritize and understand a case. It does **not** guarantee prevention, determine criminal guilt, replace incident responders, or autonomously execute high-impact containment without human approval.

## Impact pathway for Bharat

1. **Faster threat detection and triage → better digital security.** Bringing evidence into one investigation flow can help reviewers reach a timely, explainable disposition and escalate credible threats sooner. Faster triage is not the same as full incident resolution, so pilots should measure time to human-reviewed decision and the quality of that decision.
2. **Accessible workflows for SMEs → democratized cybersecurity.** Lowering the effort and cost of first-line investigation can make structured triage available to organizations that cannot operate their own SOC. Shared or service-provider delivery models could extend access, provided they protect each organization's evidence, privacy, and control.
3. **Stronger defenses for digital services and commerce → national economic impact.** More consistent incident handling can support trust in Digital India, payments, banking, and digitally enabled businesses. The long-term public benefit is a more resilient digital economy; actual fraud avoided, citizens protected, and savings must be demonstrated through measured deployments rather than inferred from transaction totals.

This is a Bharat problem because the opportunity and the exposure grow together: national-scale digital adoption, high-volume payment and messaging ecosystems, millions of resource-constrained businesses, and citizens who depend on digital services every day. An accessible, evidence-grounded investigation assistant can help close the response-capacity gap—starting with faster, reviewable phishing and cyber-incident triage, and proving its value through pilots with Indian institutions and businesses.
